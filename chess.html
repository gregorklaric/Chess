About this game

You against the computer, standard chess rules, no clock. The engine runs in the background, so the board stays responsive while it thinks.

Difficulty levels

LevelApprox. EloWhat it does
Beginner400–700Looks one move ahead with random noise in its judgement; about one move in three is a deliberately weaker choice. Hangs pieces regularly.
Improver1000–1300Looks three half-moves ahead with a little noise; about one move in eight is a weaker alternative. Sees simple tactics, misses deeper ones, and drops material now and then.
Casual1500–1700Full search, about 0.5 s per move. Tactically alert in short lines; strategically shallow.
Club1700–1900About 1.5 s per move, typically 7–9 moves (half-moves) deep.
Strong1900–2150About 4 s per move, typically 9–10 half-moves deep plus a full capture search.

These are estimates, not measured ratings, and roughly on a computer-rating-list scale; against humans in slower games they mean about a strong club player at Strong. They hold on a reasonably fast laptop or desktop. On a phone or older computer the engine searches less deeply, which costs roughly 100–200 Elo at the time-based levels.

How the computer plays

  • Openings: a built-in book of about 20 main lines (Ruy Lopez, Italian, Sicilian, French, Caro-Kann, QGD, Slav, King's Indian, Nimzo, Grünfeld, English, Réti and others), chosen at random, so games start differently. Once out of book it plays on its own judgement.
  • Strengths: short tactics, exchanges, punishing loose pieces, king attacks against a weakened pawn shield.
  • Weaknesses: long-term planning, closed positions, fortresses, and anything that only pays off beyond its search depth.
  • Endgames: plays king + pawn vs king perfectly (opposition included). It knows the standard drawn cases: wrong-coloured bishop with a rook pawn, KNN vs K, rook vs minor piece, and opposite-coloured bishops and single-rook endings as drawish. It can mate with K+Q, K+R and K+B+N. There are no endgame tablebases beyond K+P vs K.
  • It never resigns and never offers a draw.

Rules

  • Full rules: castling (including all check conditions), en passant, promotion to any piece.
  • Draws are applied automatically without a claim: stalemate, threefold repetition, the 50-move rule, and insufficient material (K vs K, K+minor vs K, K+B vs K+B with same-coloured bishops).

Controls

  • Moving: click a piece then a target square, or drag it. Dots mark legal moves, rings mark captures. On promotion, pick the piece from the menu that opens on the promotion square.
  • Undo: takes back your last move and the computer's reply. Use it freely; nothing is counted.
  • Hint: draws an arrow for a suggested move, using the Club level.
  • Move list: click any move to see that position. You can't move while reviewing; use "Back to live position" (or click the last move) to return.
  • Resign: click twice to confirm.
  • Copy FEN / Copy PGN: for analysis in another program. FEN copies the position currently shown, including a reviewed one.
  • Esc closes dialogs and cancels a promotion choice.

Saving

The game is saved in this browser automatically and continues after a reload. It is not shared between browsers or devices; clearing site data or using a private window loses it.
